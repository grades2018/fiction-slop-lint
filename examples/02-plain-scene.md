# Night Shift at the Depot

Marta checked the ledger twice before she admitted the numbers were wrong. Eleven crates were listed, nine stood on the floor, and the loading door had been locked since six. She tapped the pencil against her teeth and counted again, slower, touching each crate like a child counting stairs.

"Dov," she called. "Did the Anselm order go out?"

He appeared from behind the forklift with a thermos in his fist and grease on one cuff. "Thursday. Truck came at four, driver was in a hurry, signed without reading."

"Then somebody can't count, and I would rather it was him."

The depot smelled of cardboard and cold metal. Somewhere above them a fluorescent tube buzzed on a note just flat of middle C, and had done so for three weeks. Dov had put in a request. The request had been filed, which in his experience was a different thing from being read.

She walked the aisle to the back wall, where the missing crates should have been, and found a rectangle of cleaner concrete and a single strip of packing tape curling on the floor. Not stolen, then. Moved. Stolen goods leave a mess; moved goods leave a tidy gap and a person who thinks they are being helpful.

"Who has keys besides us?"

"Night cleaner. Her nephew, sometimes." Dov unscrewed the thermos and sniffed it. "Also the man from the insurance company, but he only comes in daylight and he is afraid of the forklift."

Marta laughed, short and surprised. Then she picked up the tape, smoothed it flat against her thumb, and read the pencilled initials on the back. P.O. Pavel Okonkwo, who ran the evening dock and had left a note on her desk that morning about a favour she had not yet read. She found it under the stapler.

It said: *Borrowed two crates for the school fair. Will return Monday. Please don't tell Dov, he'll make a speech.*

She folded the note into her pocket and said nothing at all.
